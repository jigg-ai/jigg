# MatSignal — Public Build Thesis

**Status:** DRAFT — founder review required before publishing this as a Jigg.AI article.  
**Project:** MatSignal, an experimental landing page for independent martial-arts gyms.  
**Scope:** Explain *why the experiment is worth trying*. This is not the product website copy or evidence of a working product.

## The observation

Getting someone interested in a martial-arts gym is only the first step. An inquiry may lead to a booked trial, a missed class, a first visit, a follow-up conversation, or eventually a paid membership. Each transition represents an opportunity to lose momentum.

Our initial exploration of the gym operating workflow led us to focus on that gap between **interest and membership**. The existence and financial significance of an unresolved gap at any particular gym still need to be established with actual owners; we have not measured it across the market.

## Why this might matter

For a small independent gym, members—not inquiries alone—sustain the business. Owners need to understand what happens after people express interest: did they book, attend, receive useful follow-up and ultimately enroll? An operational improvement at one of these handoffs could matter commercially, but its value cannot be assumed.

## What already exists

Gym-management software already offers booking, CRM, communications, follow-up and reporting. **MatSignal is not a proposal to replace that software.** The question is whether focusing specifically on the inquiry-to-membership journey could make existing tools more effective and reveal opportunities that ordinary CRM setup or reporting does not adequately address.

We have **not established** that current platforms are insufficient, that an additional tool is necessary, or that owners will pay for it. Those are the things to test.

## The hypothesis

A gym owner may value a focused, complementary view of the trial-to-member journey **if** it reveals a repeatable, costly problem and enables an action that the gym's existing workflow does not already handle well.

The strongest possible outcome is not an attractive dashboard. It is evidence that owners can make better decisions and convert more legitimate trial prospects into paying members at reasonable cost.

## Why start with a landing page

Before implementing CRM integrations or automation, we can present the concept honestly and invite independent gym owners to discuss the real workflow. We want to understand concrete recent cases, what their existing tools already do, and what changes they have tried.

A page that looks good is an engineering milestone, **not** proof of demand. Signups and interest are weaker evidence than an observed workflow and a genuine buyer commitment.

## What could change our minds

- Owners demonstrate that existing platforms already solve the job effectively when configured and used properly.
- The problem turns out to be sales execution or staff habits rather than missing software.
- The financial impact is too small to justify another product or the support/data-access costs.
- Qualified owners cannot be reached at an economically sensible cost.
- Owners identify a different bottleneck than the one we set out to address.

In any of these cases, the right decision may be to **revise, park or stop**, not to manufacture product differentiation.

## What this build will actually show

The public engineering record will document how we designed, implemented and tested the **landing-page experiment**, including original branding and logo design, interaction details, accessibility, form integrity, deployment tradeoffs and real costs or failures. Later results—positive, negative or inconclusive—should be compared with this thesis.

**Publication boundary:** This public thesis communicates the product rationale and falsifiable experiment. It does not disclose confidential opportunity sourcing, internal research exchanges, agent assignments, candidate rankings, private evidence archives or unreleased strategy. The public landing page speaks to gym owners; the Jigg.AI build journal explains the rationale and engineering. Both require founder review before publication.
