## The problem it started from

I train jiu-jitsu, and on most competition weekends some of my teammates are entered somewhere.
Finding out who, where and in which division means opening each event's participant list, on
whichever registration platform that organizer uses, and searching it name by name. Then again
closer to the date, because people register late, and again on the day. Nobody on the team has
the whole picture; whoever cares most does the searching, every time.

Written down, the questions were small: who from my squad is entered, at which competitions, in
which divisions, and what changed since I last looked. On the day: in what order they fight and
on which mat. Afterwards: results. **MatSignal answers the first set today.** The competition-day
and results parts aren't built.

## What it does today

- **Search** the competitions it covers by name or by team.
- **Track** people into a squad. The squad lives in your browser, with a cookie backup so Safari's
  automatic clean-up doesn't lose it. No account.
- **Read the squad as an agenda:** the next competition first, who's entered and in which
  divisions, and what's new since your last visit.
- **Matching is honest about doubt.** When an entry's name and team both match what you saved,
  it counts. When only the name matches, you're asked *It's them* or *Not them*.
- **Share one competition** as a short link. Anyone can open it without an account and sees only
  the squad's athletes entered at that competition. The link's preview names them, though how
  WhatsApp shows it hasn't been tested yet. The owner can turn a link off at any time.

## A first for this journal: a product, not a page

Builds #1 to #4 run on this site. MatSignal doesn't: it's at
[matsignal.jigg.ai](https://matsignal.jigg.ai), and nearly everything about how it's built
follows from that.

```text

  /builds/matsignal ......... this page

github.com/jigg-ai/jigg (public)
  builds/matsignal-competition/
    the build notes and the test record

MatSignal's repository (private)
  the code, its tests and the runbook

matsignal.jigg.ai (Cloudflare)
  Worker ............ pages, search, sharing, admin
  D1 ................ competitions, adult entries, shared squads
  Durable Objects ... removal requests, the search guard
  Cron .............. every minute: deletions and checks
  Access ............ the admin login
```

- **Two repositories.** This one, public, holds the journal: this page, the build notes and the
  test record. MatSignal's code is in its own private repository. It handles real people's names,
  and its defences against bulk harvesting work better unread. So this page shows the shape of the
  system, not its code.
- **Its own infrastructure.** It runs on Cloudflare Workers with a D1 database, not on Netlify like
  this site. It needs a database, a job that runs every minute, and a place to keep removal
  requests outside the database (more below). And a product deploys often: on this site every
  production deploy is metered, and MatSignal's shouldn't spend the journal's allowance.
- **A real admin login.** The admin page is behind Cloudflare Access, with a one-time code by
  email, and the Worker checks that login again itself, so a misconfigured gate stays shut.
- **It started here.** On October 30 I built a demo of it inside this site, with four made-up
  athletes. It moved out the same evening, once the work needed real data; the database and
  scheduled jobs came the next morning. The demo never merged.

## Where the entries come from

MatSignal shows entries from competitions' public participant lists. During the beta I add each
competition by hand: its public list is opened in an ordinary browser, copied, and pasted into
MatSignal's admin page. MatSignal keeps only the adult entries (name, team and division) and drops
anyone listed as under 18. Today it makes no requests of its own to the registration platforms for
these lists. That is why every competition says how old its copy is ("Copied from Smoothcomp 8 h ago"),
and why a late registration appears only after the next copy. Entries are deleted three days after
the event. Anyone can remove themselves with the form on the privacy page, and the removal holds
through later copies and database restores. Today that covers one competition: Grappling X,
Washington State, November 14.

Copying by hand is for this first, proof-of-concept phase: it was the quickest way to find out
whether the squad and the shared links are worth having, before building anything that runs on its
own. The next phase automates it. MatSignal will read the participant lists of the competitions it
covers on a schedule, under its own honest name, following each site's robots.txt and stopping when
a site refuses. Everything above still applies to what it reads: adults only, name, team and
division, deleted after the event, and removals that stick.

## Rules that shaped the build

These were fixed before any real entry went in, and most of the code follows from them.

- **Adults only, failing closed.** Only Adult and Master divisions are read. Anyone a list shows as
  under 18 is dropped, even inside an adult division, and a division the reader can't classify is
  left out.
- **Keep less.** Name, team and division. No ages, birth years, countries, photos or contact
  details, and entries go three days after the event.
- **A removal sticks.** The form hides a person at once and deletes them. The request is also kept
  outside the database, so neither a re-copied list nor a database restore can bring them back.
- **Search is for finding teammates, not harvesting.** Searches need four letters, results are
  capped, and a guard watches for sweeps and narrows what a sweeping visitor can see. Pages ask
  search engines not to index them. I'm not publishing the guard's thresholds.
- **A shared link shows only who's entered at that competition**, not the rest of the squad, and
  nothing about who opens a link is kept.

## The honest process

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="My own optimisation made the sweep guard useless" tag="my-setup">
Squad checks, the request that asks "are any of these names entered anywhere?", were charged
against the visitor's search budget for every name, every time. I changed them to charge only names
not yet checked that day, and in the same change stopped counting them against the budget shared by
everyone. When the sweep test ran again, it confirmed every name in a 2,000-person made-up roster.
Fixed before launch. The fix had a flaw of its own: one visitor could use up the shared budget for
everyone else. That was fixed too.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="A missing blank line could give a minor's name to an adult's entry" tag="my-setup">
A copied participant list arrives as plain text, one field per line. If a minor's lines ran
straight into an adult's, with no blank line between them, the reader could attach the minor's name
to the adult's entry, which is exactly the thing the adults-only rule exists to prevent. Caught in
review before launch. Now a name has to sit within two lines of its birth year, and every division
heading resets the reader.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="A saved web page imported as zero athletes" tag="my-setup">
The first import path took a saved web page. The reader found no athletes in one, and nothing
stopped an empty copy from replacing a good list. Saved pages are now refused, only copied text is
read, and a copy with no adults has to be confirmed before it replaces a list that had some.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="The review browser silently refuses dialogs" tag="tool-limit">
I review the work in the Claude desktop app's built-in browser. There, `confirm()` returns "no"
without showing anything, and clipboard writes are refused. So "Turn off link" did nothing, with no
error. The fix: questions asked inside the page, and a fallback for copying. The lesson stands:
the built-in browser is not a phone, and nothing in it reminds you of that.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="Two tests that were wrong for the wrong reasons" tag="my-setup">
The end-to-end smoke test checks that search results carry no birth years. It matched timestamps
and failed on a clean result. And a test of the removal form removed its own made-up person for
good, because removals are permanent by design, so the next run was missing a name. The smoke test
now uses fresh made-up names each run.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="Cloudflare wants a workers.dev subdomain before it will deploy" tag="tool-limit">
The first deploy failed with "You need a workers.dev subdomain", although this Worker serves only
its own domain and has workers.dev switched off. Opening Workers & Pages in the dashboard once
creates one, and the next deploy went through. Cloudflare's quirk, not Claude Code's.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="Pinning production broke local runs" tag="my-setup">
Writing the production database's id into the config pointed local runs at an empty database
("no such table"), and setting the admin login's values locked local admin. Local runs now have
their own database and their own settings, so nothing local can reach production.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="&quot;Live&quot; promised more than the page knew" tag="my-setup">
The shared page said "Live, checked 6:14 PM". The page does refresh itself, but the entries change
only when a list is copied again, so "live" was wrong exactly when it mattered: a late
registration. An outside review of the interface caught it. It now says "Page updated 6:14 PM",
next to how old the copy is.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="fail" title="A phone bug found while taking this page's screenshots" tag="my-setup">
On a phone, the shared page's header didn't wrap, so the whole page scrolled sideways. That's the
page a teammate opens from the chat, on a phone. Found while taking the screenshots above, and fixed
the same evening.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep result="pass" title="What worked">
Writing the adversarial checks early. The sweep test, the restore drill and the end-to-end smoke
test run against the Worker locally, on made-up data, and the sweep test caught the worst bug I
wrote. Two independent review passes, by agents that hadn't seen my reasoning, found more. And every decision about
data, accounts, the domain and money stayed with a human: Claude Code walked through the Cloudflare
setup step by step, while the deploy key lives in the Mac's keychain and was never printed.
</ProcessStep>

## The test

No single score: what matters here is a set of checks that each guard one promise.

<StatTiles variant="metric" stats={[
  { value: "47", label: "unit tests pass", tone: "ok" },
  { value: "57", label: "end-to-end checks pass", tone: "ok" },
  { value: "12", label: "restore-drill checks pass", tone: "ok" },

- **The smoke test** drives the product's API end to end against the local Worker, on a made-up
  competition: pages, search, the squad check, the cookie backup, the kill switch, imports and their
  safety checks, sharing, and turning links off. It doesn't click through the page itself, which is
  how the silent "Turn off link" got past it. It last ran on November 6, before the last deploy.
- **The restore drill** imitates restoring the database to before a removal (it puts the removed
  person's row back and deletes the removal record) and checks that the very next search still
  doesn't show them. Stopped shares and turned-off links get the same treatment. Run straight
  after two smoke tests, it hit a rate limit and crashed instead of failing cleanly; a minute later
  it passed. The drill should check the server's answer. **[my setup]**
- **The sweep test** runs scripted harvesting against 2,000 made-up athletes: one visitor sweeping
  every surname, a sweep spread over 200 addresses, and bulk checks of full names. An ordinary
  visitor typing a dozen names was never refused. The single-address sweeps were refused part-way,
  the sweep spread over 200 addresses was narrowed to full names only, and half the bulk checks were
  refused. But **every one of those paths still collected more than my own target of 2% of the
  roster.** Tightening the guard is open, and the per-path numbers stay private.

**Not tested yet:** real phones (Safari, Chrome, and links opened inside WhatsApp or Instagram),
a screen reader, and a competition day.

## What happens next

This is a living log. After November 14 it gets an update: whether the squad view and the shared
links held up on a real competition day, and more competitions. Then the next phase: replacing the
hand copy with MatSignal reading the lists itself, within the rules above, so that coverage stops
depending on someone pasting a list. The number that would say whether
MatSignal deserves more of my time is whether people who get a link go on to keep a squad of their
own. MatSignal doesn't count anything like that today, and whether it should is a decision still
open.
